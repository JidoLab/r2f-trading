## Script

### Hook, 0:00 to 0:25 (say this almost word for word)

This is where you currently lose 73 percent of viewers. No intro, no logo, no
greeting. Start with the chart already on screen and the failure visible.

> "This is a break of structure. You went long here, and you got stopped out
> eleven minutes later.
>
> I have watched traders do this for ten years, and it is almost never the
> setup that was wrong. Price never broke structure at all. It took liquidity
> and went the other way, and those two things look identical if you are only
> watching the wick.
>
> Three filters separate them. I use all three before I count a break as real,
> and by the end of this video you will be able to run them in about four
> seconds."

Rules for the hook:
- The chart is on screen from frame one. No talking head cold open.
- Name the loss in the first sentence. That is the retention hook.
- State the payoff before you explain anything.

### Filter 1: body close, not a wick. 0:25 to 3:00

The single most common error. A wick through a swing point is a liquidity
grab. A candle that **closes its body** beyond the point is a break.

- Show a wick-through that reversed. Mark where a trader would have entered.
- Show a body close through the same level that continued.
- Say the line plainly: "If the body did not close through it, nothing broke."

### Filter 2: displacement. 3:00 to 6:00

A real break moves with energy. It leaves a fair value gap behind it. A slow
grind through a level is price drifting, not intent.

- Show a break that left a clean FVG, and one that crawled through with
  overlapping candles.
- Point at the gap: "That gap is the evidence. Something was in a hurry."
- This is where you connect to your existing FVG video. Mention it, and put
  the link in the description and an end card.

### Filter 3: what did it break, and what was sitting there. 6:00 to 8:30

The context filter, and the one almost nobody teaches.

- If there were equal highs above the level, that was a liquidity pool, and
  the "break" was very likely a sweep of it.
- Ask the question out loud: "Was this the market breaking a level, or
  collecting the stops that were resting above it?"
- Show one of each side by side.

### Live application. 8:30 to 10:15

Take one chart, this week's if you have it, and run all three filters in real
time. Narrate the check: body close, yes. Displacement, yes. Liquidity
resting above, no. That is a break I would trade.

Then show one that fails a filter, and say you would skip it. Showing a skip
is what makes the framework credible.

### Close. 10:15 to 11:00

- Restate the three filters in one sentence each. Nothing new here.
- One instruction: "Go back through your last ten losing trades and check
  which filter each one failed. It will not be random."
- CTA, once, low key: the playbook link. Do not stack three calls to action.
